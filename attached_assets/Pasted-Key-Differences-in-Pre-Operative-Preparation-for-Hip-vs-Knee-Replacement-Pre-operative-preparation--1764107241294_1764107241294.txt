Key Differences in Pre-Operative Preparation for Hip vs. Knee Replacement
Pre-operative preparation (prehab) for both hip and knee replacements follows similar ERAS (Enhanced Recovery After Surgery) principles in the UK, as outlined by NICE guideline NG157 and the ERAS Society consensus. These focus on optimising health to reduce complications, shorten hospital stays, and improve recovery—typically starting 4-6 weeks before surgery. Core elements like smoking cessation, diabetes control, weight management, and general education apply equally to both procedures. However, subtle differences arise due to the procedures' mechanics: hip replacements often involve precautions to prevent dislocation (e.g., leg positioning), while knee replacements emphasise quadriceps strength and swelling reduction. Evidence for tailored prehab is moderate, with benefits like 20-30% lower complication rates, but it's not always procedure-specific.
